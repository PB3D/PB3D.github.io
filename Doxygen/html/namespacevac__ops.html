Detailed Description

Operations and variables pertaining to the vacuum response.

The vacuum response is calculated using the Boundary Element Method. There are two possibilities, indicated by the variable style in vac_vars.

  1. 3-D field aligned:
    • Makes use of the collocation method for the grid points along the magnetic field lines.
    • The surface integrals are taken in the parallel coordinate and the field-line label, with the trapezoidal rule along every field line, refined near the influence point, and a sum with equal weights across the field lines, which together cover the surface once (see calc_gh_1()).
    • At least 2 field lines are required, with alpha_style=2.
  2. axisymmetric:
    • Makes use of an analytical expression for the toroidal integration of the Green's functions, as shown in [3].
    • The integration in the poloidal angle is done using the collocation method.

The final matrix equation is solved through Strumpack [10], or through ScaLAPACK when PB3D was built without Strumpack.

Calculate the matrices G and H.

This is done by iterating over the subintervals, calculating the contributions to the potential values on the left and right boundary of the interval, and adding these to the appropriate values in G and H.

As each process in the blacs grid only has access to its own values, an extra interval is is calculated to the left and right of the internal process grid edges.

Optionally, this procedure can be used to calculate the coefficients G and H with respect to other points, outside of the boundary. This is useful when the potential is to be calculated at external points.

For external points no diagonal is set, and the points near the boundary are treated as follows:

  • field-line style (calc_gh_1()): the near-field rule along the field lines is used for an external point that lies closer to its nearest boundary point than that boundary point lies to its neighbour on the same field line. A point that coincides with a boundary point gets the analytical half-cell integral of G, with the metric factors of the left endpoint of the interval, which is the coinciding point or its neighbour on the field line.
  • axisymmetric style (calc_gh_2()): a point that coincides with a boundary point gets the row that returns the boundary value, with \(4\pi\) in the column of that point and zero elsewhere. Other points near the boundary get no special treatment.

Within a few grid spacings of the boundary the quadrature does not resolve the kernels (eval_pot_ext()).

Note
Multiple field lines are stored sequentially, which implies that the integral between the last point on a field line and the first point on the next field lines needs to be left out.
For style 1 the kernel is integrated along the field line within four interval lengths of the influence point (vac_utilities.calc_gh_int_1), because neighbouring field lines can lie much closer together than the points along them. The rule along and across the field lines is described in calc_gh_1().

integer function vac_ops::calc_gh_1 ( type(vac_type), intent(inout) vac,
integer, intent(in) n_r_in,
integer, dimension(:,:), intent(in) lims_r_in,
real(dp), dimension(:,:), intent(in) x_vec_in,
real(dp), dimension(:,:), intent(in), pointer g_in,
real(dp), dimension(:,:), intent(in), pointer h_in,

Calculates matrices G and H in 3-D configuration.

The boundary points lie on \(n_\alpha\) field lines with the labels \(\alpha_k = \alpha_\text{min} + (k-1)\Delta\alpha\), \(k = 1,\ldots,n_\alpha\). Every field line has \(n_p\) points \(p_l = p_\text{min} + (l-1)\Delta p\), \(l = 1,\ldots,n_p\), in the parallel coordinate \(p\). The steps are \(\Delta\alpha = (\alpha_\text{max}-\alpha_\text{min})/n_\alpha\) and \(\Delta p = (p_\text{max}-p_\text{min})/(n_p-1)\) (vac_utilities.vac_steps()): the grid in \(\alpha\) leaves out \(\alpha_\text{max}\), which is the field line at \(\alpha_\text{min}\) again, while every field line includes both of its end points. The points are stored line by line: point \(l\) of field line \(k\) is column \(j = (k-1)n_p + l\) of G and H.

A row of G and H belongs to an influence point \(\mathbf{r}_i\), the point at which the potential is evaluated. A column belongs to a boundary point \(\mathbf{r}_j\), over which the surface integral runs.

The surface integrals are taken in the coordinates \((p,\alpha)\). With alpha_style 2 the parallel range is one turn, \(p_\text{max}-p_\text{min} = 2\pi\), and the field lines have to cover one period of the label, \(\alpha_\text{max}-\alpha_\text{min} = 2\pi\) (the default). The rectangle in \((p,\alpha)\) then covers the surface once. The stored normal \(\mathbf{N} = -\mathcal{J}\nabla\psi\) (vac%norm) has the length of the surface element per unit \(p\) and per unit \(\alpha\), \(|\mathbf{N}| = |\partial_\alpha\mathbf{r}\times\partial_p\mathbf{r}|\), so the weights below contain no other Jacobian.

Far from the influence point, the rule is

\[ \iint f \, \text{d}p \, \text{d}\alpha \approx \sum_{k=1}^{n_\alpha} \Delta\alpha \sum_{l=1}^{n_p} c_l \, \Delta p \, f(p_l,\alpha_k), \qquad c_1 = c_{n_p} = \tfrac{1}{2}, \quad c_l = 1 \text{ otherwise}, \]

where \(f\) is the integrand of the G term or of the H term of row \(i\).

  • Along each field line this is the trapezoidal rule. The loop over the intervals \([p_l,p_{l+1}]\) multiplies the kernel at both end points of every interval by \(\Delta p\,\Delta\alpha/2\) and adds it to their columns. A point inside a field line therefore collects the weight \(\Delta p\,\Delta\alpha\), and the first and the last point of a field line collect \(\Delta p\,\Delta\alpha/2\). The last point of one field line and the first point of the next are neighbours in the storage only, and the loop skips that pair.
  • Across the field lines it is a sum with the equal weight \(\Delta\alpha\), which is the trapezoidal rule for a function that is periodic in \(\alpha\). The kernel is evaluated on the field lines only; nothing is interpolated between them.

Near the influence point, vac_utilities.calc_gh_int_1() changes the rule along the field line and leaves the sum across the field lines as it is. Within four interval lengths of the influence point it integrates the kernel over each half-interval with Gauss-Legendre points on the cubic through four points of the same field line; for the first and the last interval of a field line these four points lie on one side of the interval. For an influence point off the boundary this is done only if the point lies closer to its nearest boundary point than that boundary point lies to its neighbour on the same field line. At the singular point it uses the analytical integral over the half-cell, which reaches \(\Delta\alpha/2\) to either side of the field line. That integral and the cell integral of the direct diagonal of H (vac_utilities.calc_diag_direct(), only with vac_diag_style 2) are the only places where the kernel is integrated across the field lines.

◆ calc_vac_res()

integer function, public vac_ops::calc_vac_res ( type(modes_type), intent(in) mds,
type(vac_type), intent(inout) vac )

Calculates the vacuum response.

First G and H are completed if this is not the first Richardson level.

Then the vacuum contribution is calculated using the two-fold strategy of first solving \(\overline{\text{H}}\overline{\Phi} = \overline{\text{G}}\overline{\text{E}} \overline{\text{P}}\), followed by left-multiplication of \(\overline{\Phi}\) by \(\overline{\text{P}}\overline{\text{E}}^\dagger\).

The non-square matrix \(\overline{\text{E}}\) contains the exponents for different mode numbers and different poloidal grid points, whereas \(\overline{\text{P}}\) are diagonal matrices that contain the factors \((nq-m)\).

In practice, the complex matrix \(\overline{\text{E}}\) is split in the two components of a real matrix twice the width.

For vacuum style 1, the parallel coordinate and the field-line label of every boundary point follow from min_par_X, min_alpha and the steps of vac_utilities.vac_steps(); for vacuum style 2, the poloidal angles are those stored in vac%ang.

If jump_to_sol is used for the current Richardson level, the vacuum quantities are not calculated, but just restored.

Potential at points off the boundary, from its values and its normal data on the boundary.

The kernels are taken along the stored normal \(-\mathcal{J}\nabla\psi\), which points out of the vacuum. Green's second identity then gives, at a point \(\vec{x}\) off the boundary,

\[ \sum_j \left( H(\vec{x},\vec{x}_j)\phi_j - G(\vec{x},\vec{x}_j)\,\text{d}\phi_j \right) = \left\{ \begin{array}{ll} 4\pi\phi(\vec{x}) & \text{in the vacuum,} \\ 0 & \text{in the plasma} \end{array} \right. \]

for a potential \(\phi\) that is harmonic in the vacuum and decays at infinity, and \(0\) in the vacuum and \(-4\pi\phi(\vec{x})\) in the plasma for a potential harmonic inside the boundary (the constant for the field-line style, \(R^{|n|}\) for the axisymmetric one). On the boundary the same sum, with the diagonal that calc_GH() adds there, is the identity \(\overline{\text{H}}\phi - \overline{\text{G}}\,\text{d}\phi = c\phi\) of solve_Phi_BEM(), \(c = 0\) and \(-4\pi\) for the two kinds.

This routine returns the sum divided by \(4\pi\) for every column of Phi_s and D_s. The rows of G and H are assembled by calc_GH() for the external points (no diagonal; the trapezoidal rule with the near-field rule along the field lines for the field-line style, the toroidal-function kernels for the axisymmetric style), in chunks that bound the memory. Within a few grid spacings of the boundary the quadrature does not resolve the kernels.

Every process of the BLACS grid of the vacuum has to call it.

Calculates potential Phi on boundary of BEM in terms of some right-hand side .

This is done by solving the system of equations \(\overline{\text{H}}\overline{\Phi} = \overline{\text{G}}\overline{\text{R}}\), where \(\overline{\text{R}}\) is the right-hand side, for example equal to \(\overline{\text{E}}\overline{\text{P}}\) to solve in function of the individual Fourier modes.

Both vacuum styles assemble \(\overline{\text{H}}\) in the vacuum-side convention: the kernels take the normal derivative along the stored normal \(-\mathcal{J}\nabla\psi\), out of the vacuum, and the diagonal carries \(-2\beta\) with \(\beta\) the vacuum's internal angle (the row-sum identity for the field-line style). Potentials that are harmonic in the vacuum and decay at infinity then satisfy \(\overline{\text{H}}\overline{\phi} = \overline{\text{G}}\overline{\text{D}\phi}\), and potentials that are harmonic inside the boundary satisfy \(\overline{\text{H}}\overline{\phi} - \overline{\text{G}}\overline{\text{D}\phi} = -4\pi\overline{\phi}\) (both verified in the full-stack tests). The corner term also makes the system nonsingular when the first and last points of a closed boundary coincide, as they do for HELENA: the two rows differ only in the column of that term.

The linear system is solved with STRUMPack when PB3D was configured with it, and with ScaLAPACK LU (pdgesv) otherwise.

Stores calculation of the vacuum response by storing the necessary variables.

This is done by the last process, which is the one that contains the variables at the plasma edge, and then this is broadcasted to the other processes.

The workings of this routine are summarized in the following diagram for VMEC:

Before storing the new variables, the procedure checks the following things:

  • For the first equilibrium job, this routine copies the results from the previous Richardson level into the appropriate subranges of the new vacuum variables if no restart of Richardson levels was done.
  • If a restart was done and the level is greater than one, there is a reconstruction of the previous level's variables. But this will happen in calc_vac_res(), not in this procedure.

If there is a jump straight to the solution, however, the procedure returns after reconstructing the current level's variables.

If the previous G and H variables are empty, they are regenerated later, in calc_gh(). This indicates that a reconstruction happened, either because a Richardson restart was performed, or because a new Richardson level was started after a previous level in which it was jumped straight to the solution.

For HELENA, there is only 1 equilibrium job at the first Richardson level, and the vacuum has to be calculated only once then. If there is a jump to solution or if there is Richardson restart, it only needs to be reconstructed.

◆ vac_pot_plot()

integer function, public vac_ops::vac_pot_plot ( type(grid_type), intent(in) grid_sol,
type(vac_type), intent(inout) vac,
type(sol_type), intent(in) sol,

Plots the vacuum potential of computed eigenmodes outside the plasma.

For every eigenmode of X_ids the normal data of the vacuum potential on the boundary are those of calc_vac_res(): along the stored normal \(-\mathcal{J}\nabla\psi\), out of the vacuum, \(\text{d}\phi = -\mathcal{J}\vec{B}\cdot\nabla X = -i(nq-m)X\), summed over the modes of the solution at the plasma edge with the basis \(e^{i(n\alpha + (nq-m)\theta)} = e^{i(n\zeta_\text{F} - m\theta)}\) of the field-line style, or \(e^{-im\theta}\) for the axisymmetric style, which splits off \(e^{in\zeta_\text{F}}\). These data are \(-\mathcal{J}Q_n\), with \(Q_n\) the normal component of the magnetic perturbation that POST plots: the normal component of the perturbed field is continuous across the boundary. The boundary potential follows from the exterior system of solve_Phi_BEM(), the potential in the vacuum from Green's representation (eval_pot_ext()): for the field-line style the complex potential at the 3-D points, for the axisymmetric style \(\hat{\phi}(R,Z)\) times \(e^{in\zeta_\text{F}}\).

The points lie in the planes of constant \(\zeta_\text{F}\) of the plasma plots, n_zeta_plot values from min_zeta_plot to max_zeta_plot in units of \(\pi\) (the geometrical angle is \(-\zeta_\text{F}\) for VMEC and HELENA), on a grid of n_vac_plot points in \(R\) and \(Z\) from min_Rvac_plot to max_Rvac_plot and from min_Zvac_plot to max_Zvac_plot, in metres. Where a maximum does not exceed its minimum (the default), the range is that of the boundary points widened on both sides by half the larger of their widths in \(R\) and \(Z\).

Written for every eigenmode: the real and imaginary parts of the potential at time zero, [id]_vac_pot_RE and [id]_vac_pot_IM (HDF5), and the boundary points with their normal data and potential, Data/[id]_vac_bnd.dat. Written once: vac_in_plasma, 1 inside the plasma and 0 in the vacuum (for the field-line style the side of the normal of the nearest boundary point close to it and minus the representation of the constant farther out, for the axisymmetric style the boundary polygon); the potential is set to zero there. Near the boundary the quadrature does not resolve the kernels; sub_nearest() removes the largest part of that error (check_vac_exact() prints the error against the distance, with and without it).

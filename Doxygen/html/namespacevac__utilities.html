Two variants, both with the cell integral of the kernel (H_sing_node):

  • smooth: \(-2\pi\) for the corner term, the solid angle of the vacuum at a point of the smooth surface, consistent with the kernels of calc_GH_int_1, which are evaluated on the smooth surface;

Calculate G_ij and H_ij on an interval for field line aligned configurations.

The source variables are given on a stencil of four consecutive points of the field line, [1:4,dim] with dim the Cartesian dimension; the interval is the one between stencil points i_int and i_int+1. Each endpoint receives the kernel integrated over its half of the interval, normalized to the half-cell weight \(\Delta p \Delta a / 2\) that calc_GH_1 applies, so that a constant kernel returns its value.

Far from the influence point, more than n_near interval lengths away, the kernel value at the endpoint is used, which amounts to the trapezoidal rule along the field line. Closer, the kernel is integrated over the half-interval with 8-point Gauss-Legendre on the cubic interpolant of position and normal through the stencil. This resolves the variation of the kernel along a neighbouring field line: where the field lines run almost toroidally, as at the bean tips of W7-X, they are packed far closer (0.007 \(R_0\)) than the points along them (0.06 \(R_0\)), the kernel seen along the neighbouring line is a peak much narrower than the point spacing, and sampling it at the points gives errors of order one whose sign depends on how the points of neighbouring lines happen to line up. The stencil and the Gauss points lie on one field line, so this rule changes the integral along the line only; across the field lines calc_GH_1 keeps the sum with the weight \(\Delta a\) per field line.

For subintegrals adjacent to the singularity (i.e. with the influence point as one of their endpoints), the contribution of the singular endpoint is replaced by the analytical integral of the kernel over its grid half-cell. In field-aligned coordinates, with \(\delta p\) along the field line (metric \(g_{\theta\theta}\) = h_fac(3)) and \(\delta a\) the field line label (metric \(g_{\alpha\alpha}\) = h_fac(1), coupling \(g_{\alpha\theta}\) = h_fac(2)), the squared distance near the singular point is the quadratic form \( g_{\theta\theta} \delta p^2 + 2 g_{\alpha\theta} \delta p \delta a + g_{\alpha\alpha} \delta a^2 \), and with the metric-normalized half-cell sizes \(U = \sqrt{g_{\theta \theta}} \Delta p/2\), \(V = \sqrt{g_{\alpha\alpha}} \Delta a/2\) and nonorthogonality \(s = g_{\alpha\theta} / \sqrt{g_{\alpha\alpha} g_{\theta\theta}}\), the exact primitive is

\[ \int_0^U \int_{-V}^{V} \frac{\text{d}u \text{d}v} {\sqrt{u^2+2suv+v^2}} = U \ln\left(\frac{V+sU+D_+}{-V+sU+D_-}\right) + V \ln\left(\frac{U+sV+D_+}{-U+sV+D_-}\right), \quad D_\pm = \sqrt{U^2 \pm 2sUV + V^2}. \]

In the same units as the regular kernel values, the singular endpoint contribution is then \(-2 F / (\Delta p \Delta a \sqrt{g_{\alpha\alpha} g_{\theta\theta}}) \).

(Verified against brute-force quadrature in the full-stack tests.)

Note
This routine does not calculate the contribution \(2\beta\) nor the cell integral of the H kernel at the singular point: vac_ops.calc_gh() sets the diagonals afterwards. By default both come from the identities of vac_ops.diag_indirect(), which replace the half-cell value of G computed here; with vac_diag_style 2 the diagonal of H comes from calc_diag_direct() and G keeps the half-cell value.
With use_near false the near-field rule is not used. For points off the boundary (vac_ops.eval_pot_ext()) it pays only within about an interval length of the boundary: farther out the trapezoidal rule along the lines and across them is more accurate, because the errors of the trapezoidal rule at the ends of the lines cancel between the lines, whose ends interleave, while the edge of the near-field zone leaves an error of second order in the step along the lines (on the circular VMEC twin at 64 lines of 100 points: 1e-3 of the potential at half a minor radius from the boundary, against 1e-7 with the trapezoidal rule alone).

Calculate G_ij and H_ij on an interval for axisymmetric configurations.

The indices for the source variables are [left:right,dim] where dim is the Cartesian dimension. The same holds for ql.

For subintegrals close to the singularity (i.e. where the tolerance is no being met), the routine approximates the toroidal functions with the analytical approximation. If one of the boundaries of the subintegral is far enough from the singularity, there is an additional contribution due to the difference between the actual toroidal function and the approximation. This contribution is zero if both boundaries are close, because in this case it is assumed that the entire integral is given by the analytical approximation.

Note
The kernels are those of eqs. (130)-(131) of the 2019 manuscript with the sign of H reversed (the leading minus signs of the three H formulas): the normal derivative is taken along the stored normal \(-\mathcal{J}\nabla\psi\), out of the vacuum, for both vacuum styles (erratum E1 of that manuscript). This routine does not calculate the corner term: calc_GH_2 adds \(-2\beta\) to the diagonal of H, with \(\beta\) the vacuum's internal angle on the vacuum side.

Fits diagonal entries of H and G to Green's identity for potentials whose boundary values and normal data are known.

For a potential \(\phi_k\) with normal data \(\phi'_k\), row \(i\) of the identity is

\[ H_{ii}\phi_{k,i} - G_{ii}\phi'_{k,i} = r_{k,i} \equiv c_k\phi_{k,i} - \sum_{j\ne i} H_{ij}\phi_{k,j} + \sum_{j\ne i} G_{ij}\phi'_{k,j} , \]

where the free term \(c_k\) is \(-4\pi\) for a potential that is harmonic inside the boundary and \(0\) for one that is harmonic outside it and decays. The off-diagonal sums are the products with the full matrices minus their current diagonals, so the matrices are not changed.

Either one entry is fitted, fit_H or fit_G, in the least-squares sense over any number of potentials; the other keeps the value that dH or dG holds on input, and its term moves to the right-hand side. Or both are fitted from exactly two potentials, whose two equations per row are solved exactly:

\[ H_{ii} = \frac{r_{1,i}\phi'_{2,i} - r_{2,i}\phi'_{1,i}}{D_i}, \quad G_{ii} = \frac{r_{1,i}\phi_{2,i} - r_{2,i}\phi_{1,i}}{D_i}, \quad D_i = \phi_{1,i}\phi'_{2,i} - \phi'_{1,i}\phi_{2,i} . \]

The ratio \(s_i = |D_i| / (|\phi_{1,i}\phi'_{2,i}| + |\phi'_{1,i}\phi_{2,i}|)\), between 0 and 1, does not depend on how either potential is scaled; about \(-\log_{10}s_i\) digits of \(D_i\) are lost to cancellation. The routine stops with an error when \(s_i < 10^{-6}\) at some row, where the two equations cannot separate \(H_{ii}\) from \(G_{ii}\), and warns when the smallest \(s_i\) is below \(10^{-2}\).

Parameters
[in]vacvacuum variables
[in]phipotentials at the boundary points, one column per potential
[in]dphitheir normal data
[in]c_freefree term of the identity of each potential
[in]fit_hfit the diagonal of H
[in]fit_gfit the diagonal of G
[in,out]dhdiagonal of H (global): known on input, or fitted on output
[in,out]dgdiagonal of G (global): known on input, or fitted on output

Integral of the H kernel over the cell of a node of the field-line mesh.

The kernel \(\mathbf{n}(\mathbf{r})\cdot(\mathbf{r}-\mathbf{r}_0)/ |\mathbf{r}-\mathbf{r}_0|^3\) (with \(\mathbf{n}\) the stored normal \(-\mathcal{J}\nabla\psi\) at the source) is bounded on the surface near its own node, because the surface leaves its tangent plane at second order: to leading order \(\mathbf{n}(\mathbf{r})\cdot (\mathbf{r}-\mathbf{r}_0) = -\tfrac12\, \mathbf{n}_0\cdot\partial_j \mathbf{e}_k\, \Delta u^j \Delta u^k\) ( \(j,k\) the parallel and field-line coordinates). Over the cell of the node, \(|\theta-\theta_0| \le \Delta\theta/2\), \(|\alpha-\alpha_0| \le \Delta\alpha/2\), in the local polar coordinates of eqs. (49)-(72) of the 2019 manuscript (with \(A = g_{\alpha\theta}/|\mathbf{n}|\), \(B = g_{\alpha\alpha}/ |\mathbf{n}|\) and \(\rho(\beta)\) the radius of the cell), the integral is

\[ I = -\frac{\sqrt{g_{\alpha\alpha}}}{2|\mathbf{n}|^2} \int_{-\pi/2}^{\pi/2} q(\beta)\,\rho(\beta)\,\mathrm{d}\beta, \quad q = \mathbf{n}_0\cdot\partial_j\mathbf{e}_k\, t^j t^k, \quad t = \left(\cos\beta, \frac{\sin\beta - A\cos\beta}{B}\right), \]

(with \(A\) and \(B\) taken positive: the mirror cell, the frame of the opposite orientation, is an isometric image and gives the same integral), which is the corrected eq. (69) of the manuscript (erratum E22: the printed one has a wrong factor and only the umbilic part of the second fundamental form). The half-cell of the manuscript equals the full cell of the node for this kernel. Verified against direct integration on sphere and torus nodes (first-order convergence), and by the full-stack test H_sing_node.

[out]i_singintegral of the kernel over the cell

pure subroutine, public vac_utilities::solid_angle_node ( real(dp), dimension(4,3), intent(in) u,
real(dp), dimension(3), intent(in) n_hat,
real(dp), intent(out) omega )

Solid angle at a node of the mesh, on the side of n_hat.

The surface of the mesh at a node is the cone of four flat facets, one between each pair of edges that are consecutive in azimuth about n_hat. The solid angle of the region on the n_hat side of all four facets is eq. (45) of the 2019 manuscript on the vacuum energy (exact; not its small-tilt form (46), which fails on skewed meshes, erratum E6): with the facet normals \(\mathbf{a}_i\) tilted by \(\beta_i\) from n_hat towards the azimuth \(\bar\varphi_i\), and \(\varphi_i\) the azimuths of the edges,

\[ \Omega = 2\pi + \sum_{i=1}^4 \left[\sin^{-1}\left(\sin\beta_i \sin(\varphi-\bar\varphi_i)\right)\right]_{\varphi_i}^{\varphi_{i+1}} . \]

The edges may be given in any order. With n_hat the unit normal into the plasma, the result is the plasma's solid angle \(\Omega_p\) and the vacuum's is \(4\pi - \Omega_p\).

Parameters
[in]uthe four edge vectors from the node to its neighbours
[in]n_hatunit normal of the side whose solid angle is returned
[out]omegasolid angle on that side

Direct diagonals of H for the field-\/line vacuum. 

Two variants, both with the cell integral of the kernel (H\+\_\+sing\+\_\+node):
\begin{DoxyItemize}
\item smooth: $-2\pi$ for the corner term, the solid angle of the vacuum at a point of the smooth surface, consistent with the kernels of calc\+\_\+\+GH\+\_\+int\+\_\+1, which are evaluated on the smooth surface;
\item faceted: minus the vacuum\textquotesingle{}s solid angle at the node of the faceted surface, eq. (45) of the 2019 manuscript (solid\+\_\+angle\+\_\+node), which belongs to a discretization with flat elements; on a skewed mesh, such as the field-\/aligned one, the facets tilt strongly (erratum E6) and this value is far from $2\pi$. The four edges run to the neighbours along the field line and on the neighbouring lines (periodic in the field-\/line label). The second derivatives of the position come from second differences of the stored points. The end points of the field lines have only three edges and half a cell, and are left out ({\ttfamily avail} false).

subroutine, public vac\+\_\+utilities::\+calc\+\_\+gh\+\_\+int\+\_\+1 (\begin{DoxyParamCaption}\item[{real(dp), dimension(2), intent(inout)}]{g}{, }\item[{real(dp), dimension(2), intent(inout)}]{h}{, }\item[{real(dp), dimension(4,3), intent(in)}]{x\+\_\+s}{, }\item[{real(dp), dimension(3), intent(in)}]{x\+\_\+in}{, }\item[{real(dp), dimension(4,3), intent(in)}]{norm\+\_\+s}{, }\item[{real(dp), dimension(3), intent(in)}]{h\+\_\+fac\+\_\+in}{, }\item[{real(dp), dimension(2), intent(in)}]{step\+\_\+size}{, }\item[{real(dp), intent(in)}]{tol}{, }\item[{integer, intent(in)}]{i\+\_\+int}{, }\item[{logical, intent(in), optional}]{use\+\_\+near}{}\end{DoxyParamCaption})}



Calculate G\+\_\+ij and H\+\_\+ij on an interval for field line aligned configurations. 

The source variables are given on a stencil of four consecutive points of the field line, {\ttfamily \+[1:4,dim]} with {\ttfamily dim} the Cartesian dimension; the interval is the one between stencil points {\ttfamily i\+\_\+int} and {\ttfamily i\+\_\+int+1}. Each endpoint receives the kernel integrated over its half of the interval, normalized to the half-\/cell weight  $\Delta p  \Delta a /
        2$ that calc\+\_\+\+GH\+\_\+1 applies, so that a constant kernel returns its value.

Far from the influence point, more than {\ttfamily n\+\_\+near} interval lengths away, the kernel value at the endpoint is used, which amounts to the trapezoidal rule along the field line. Closer, the kernel is integrated over the half-\/interval with 8-\/point Gauss-\/\+Legendre on the cubic interpolant of position and normal through the stencil. This resolves the variation of the kernel along a neighbouring field line: where the field lines run almost toroidally, as at the bean tips of W7-\/X, they are packed far closer (0.\+007 $R_0$) than the points along them (0.\+06 $R_0$), the kernel seen along the neighbouring line is a peak much narrower than the point spacing, and sampling it at the points gives errors of order one whose sign depends on how the points of neighbouring lines happen to line up. The stencil and the Gauss points lie on one field line, so this rule changes the integral along the line only; across the field lines calc\+\_\+\+GH\+\_\+1 keeps the sum with the weight $\Delta a$ per field line.

For subintegrals adjacent to the singularity (i.\+e. with the influence point as one of their endpoints), the contribution of the singular endpoint is replaced by the analytical integral of the kernel over its grid half-\/cell. In field-\/aligned coordinates, with $\delta p$ along the field line (metric $g_{\theta\theta}$ = {\ttfamily h\+\_\+fac(3)}) and $\delta a$ the field line label (metric $g_{\alpha\alpha}$ = {\ttfamily h\+\_\+fac(1)}, coupling $g_{\alpha\theta}$ = {\ttfamily h\+\_\+fac(2)}), the squared distance near the singular point is the quadratic form  $ g_{\theta\theta} \delta p^2 + 2 g_{\alpha\theta} \delta p \delta a
         + g_{\alpha\alpha} \delta a^2 $, and with the metric-\/normalized half-\/cell sizes  $U = \sqrt{g_{\theta
        \theta}} \Delta p/2$, $V = \sqrt{g_{\alpha\alpha}} \Delta a/2$ and nonorthogonality  $s  = g_{\alpha\theta}  /  \sqrt{g_{\alpha\alpha}
        g_{\theta\theta}}$, the exact primitive is     \[ \int_0^U \int_{-V}^{V} \frac{\text{d}u \text{d}v}
         {\sqrt{u^2+2suv+v^2}} =
         U \ln\left(\frac{V+sU+D_+}{-V+sU+D_-}\right) +
         V \ln\left(\frac{U+sV+D_+}{-U+sV+D_-}\right), \quad
         D_\pm = \sqrt{U^2 \pm 2sUV + V^2}. \] In the same units as the regular kernel values, the singular endpoint contribution is then  $-2 F / (\Delta p \Delta a \sqrt{g_{\alpha\alpha} g_{\theta\theta}})
$.

(Verified against brute-\/force quadrature in the full-\/stack tests.)

\begin{DoxyNote}{Note}
This routine does not calculate the contribution $2\beta$ nor the cell integral of the H kernel at the singular point: \doxylink{namespacevac__ops_a8d70ea940b7548e4a2f397699c694b67}{vac\+\_\+ops.\+calc\+\_\+gh()} sets the diagonals afterwards. By default both come from the identities of vac\+\_\+ops.\+diag\+\_\+indirect(), which replace the half-\/cell value of G computed here; with {\ttfamily vac\+\_\+diag\+\_\+style} 2 the diagonal of H comes from \doxylink{namespacevac__utilities_a3c3d0d491021b5c7ebb76c977eaf1da2}{calc\+\_\+diag\+\_\+direct()} and G keeps the half-\/cell value.

With {\ttfamily use\+\_\+near} false the near-\/field rule is not used. For points off the boundary (\doxylink{namespacevac__ops_a33bf7285ead934fa8e4242c708542d92}{vac\+\_\+ops.\+eval\+\_\+pot\+\_\+ext()}) it pays only within about an interval length of the boundary: farther out the trapezoidal rule along the lines and across them is more accurate, because the errors of the trapezoidal rule at the ends of the lines cancel between the lines, whose ends interleave, while the edge of the near-\/field zone leaves an error of second order in the step along the lines (on the circular VMEC twin at 64 lines of 100 points: 1e-\/3 of the potential at half a minor radius from the boundary, against 1e-\/7 with the trapezoidal rule alone). 
\end{DoxyNote}

Calculate G\+\_\+ij and H\+\_\+ij on an interval for axisymmetric configurations. 

The indices for the source variables are {\ttfamily \+[left:right,dim]} where {\ttfamily dim} is the Cartesian dimension. The same holds for {\ttfamily ql}.

For subintegrals close to the singularity (i.\+e. where the tolerance is no being met), the routine approximates the toroidal functions with the analytical approximation. If one of the boundaries of the subintegral is far enough from the singularity, there is an additional contribution due to the difference between the actual toroidal function and the approximation. This contribution is zero if both boundaries are close, because in this case it is assumed that the entire integral is given by the analytical approximation.

\begin{DoxyNote}{Note}
The kernels are those of eqs. (130)-\/(131) of the 2019 manuscript with the sign of H reversed (the leading minus signs of the three H formulas): the normal derivative is taken along the stored normal $-\mathcal{J}\nabla\psi$, out of the vacuum, for both vacuum styles (erratum E1 of that manuscript). This routine does not calculate the corner term: calc\+\_\+\+GH\+\_\+2 adds $-2\beta$ to the diagonal of H, with $\beta$ the vacuum\textquotesingle{}s internal angle on the vacuum side. 
\end{DoxyNote}

integer function, public vac\+\_\+utilities::\+fit\+\_\+diag (\begin{DoxyParamCaption}\item[{type(\doxymbox{\hyperlink{structvac__vars_1_1vac__type}{vac\+\_\+type}}), intent(in)}]{vac}{, }\item[{real(dp), dimension(:\+,:\+), intent(in)}]{phi}{, }\item[{real(dp), dimension(:\+,:\+), intent(in)}]{dphi}{, }\item[{real(dp), dimension(:\+), intent(in)}]{c\+\_\+free}{, }\item[{logical, intent(in)}]{fit\+\_\+h}{, }\item[{logical, intent(in)}]{fit\+\_\+g}{, }\item[{real(dp), dimension(:\+), intent(inout)}]{dh}{, }\item[{real(dp), dimension(:\+), intent(inout)}]{dg}{}\end{DoxyParamCaption})}



Fits diagonal entries of H and G to Green\textquotesingle{}s identity for potentials whose boundary values and normal data are known. 

For a potential $\phi_k$ with normal data $\phi\textnormal{\textquotesingle}_k$, row $i$ of the identity is  \[ H_{ii}\phi_{k,i} - G_{ii}\phi\textnormal{\textquotesingle}_{k,i} = r_{k,i} \equiv c_k\phi_{k,i}
         - \sum_{j\ne i} H_{ij}\phi_{k,j} + \sum_{j\ne i} G_{ij}\phi\textnormal{\textquotesingle}_{k,j} , \] where the free term $c_k$ is $-4\pi$ for a potential that is harmonic inside the boundary and $0$ for one that is harmonic outside it and decays. The off-\/diagonal sums are the products with the full matrices minus their current diagonals, so the matrices are not changed.

Either one entry is fitted, {\ttfamily fit\+\_\+H} or {\ttfamily fit\+\_\+G}, in the least-\/squares sense over any number of potentials; the other keeps the value that {\ttfamily dH} or {\ttfamily dG} holds on input, and its term moves to the right-\/hand side. Or both are fitted from exactly two potentials, whose two equations per row are solved exactly:   \[ H_{ii} = \frac{r_{1,i}\phi\textnormal{\textquotesingle}_{2,i} - r_{2,i}\phi\textnormal{\textquotesingle}_{1,i}}{D_i}, \quad
         G_{ii} = \frac{r_{1,i}\phi_{2,i} - r_{2,i}\phi_{1,i}}{D_i}, \quad
         D_i = \phi_{1,i}\phi\textnormal{\textquotesingle}_{2,i} - \phi\textnormal{\textquotesingle}_{1,i}\phi_{2,i} . \] The ratio  $s_i = |D_i| / (|\phi_{1,i}\phi\textnormal{\textquotesingle}_{2,i}| +
        |\phi\textnormal{\textquotesingle}_{1,i}\phi_{2,i}|)$, between 0 and 1, does not depend on how either potential is scaled; about $-\log_{10}s_i$ digits of $D_i$ are lost to cancellation. The routine stops with an error when $s_i < 10^{-6}$ at some row, where the two equations cannot separate $H_{ii}$ from $G_{ii}$, and warns when the smallest $s_i$ is below $10^{-2}$.
